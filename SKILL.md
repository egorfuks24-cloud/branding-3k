# creator1x · Branding: three K

Build a visual argument before choosing decoration. A convincing explanation must describe a visible decision grounded in the business; a plausible story invented after styling is not evidence.

The source video distinguishes **content** (what is literally shown), **context** (how it should feel) and **creative** (the specific business characteristic expressed visually). Read [video-principles.md](references/video-principles.md) for source boundaries and the boxing example. The workflow below is an original operationalization, not the speaker's undisclosed questionnaire.

Use the depth the task needs. For a new brand or substantial redesign, establish all three layers before styling. For an approved direction, restore its rationale and implement the requested change; do not restart discovery or replace the identity. A narrow correction does not require a new three-K board.

## 1. Restore the actual brief

Read existing decisions, rejected designs and real assets. Reuse answers rather than restarting discovery. Establish product, audience, main action, channels, fixed content, and what the user rejects. Ask only about missing facts that materially change the result. If no business distinction is established, mark candidate meanings as hypotheses; do not fabricate founder stories, values, traction or competitive uniqueness.

## 2. Build a concise three-K board

Keep this working document outside the customer-facing product:

| Layer | Record | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Content | Actual objects, people, products, data and necessary controls | User brief, product, provided assets |
| Context | A few precise sensations and the visual means that express them | User references, audience/use situation |
| Creative | A specific mechanism, material, tension or story belonging to this business | Verified fact or explicitly labelled hypothesis |

For an unresolved layer, use [discovery-prompts.md](references/discovery-prompts.md) selectively. Adjectives such as premium, modern and unique are not creative mechanisms. Category cues alone establish content/context; they do not explain this brand.

## 3. Translate meaning into a visual system

Write the chain **business cue → meaning → visual decision → applications → observable check** for each defining choice. Separate facts from proposed interpretation.

Develop composition, imagery, typography, color roles, material and interaction together. A palette is not an identity. Establish a reusable visual signature that has a useful role in the primary action, rather than a texture confined to the cover. Avoid forcing the boxing case's dots, yellow, condensed lettering, blur or timers onto unrelated businesses.

Study the user's specific references visually. Record the feature borrowed and how it is transformed, without copying another brand's assets or identity. Sources are evidence for a principle, not retrospective justification for a familiar template. Check actual font files/language support and asset rights.

Vary color by meaningful role or collection; adding more colors randomly does not solve sameness. Preserve readable controls and the integrity of product imagery. Material effects must not disguise poor cropping, stretched art or weak structure.

### For websites and apps

Make the system explain what the user can get or do. Use real content in the entrance, selection/detail view and primary action. The same brand principle must work inside the product, not only on a hero image.

- **Content:** show enough representative material to understand the offer; make contents, format and selection clear where relevant. Distinguish product previews from delivered files.
- **Context:** translate the agreed feeling into composition, density, image treatment and material. Preserve legibility and practical use.
- **Creative:** connect a real product characteristic to a useful organizing or interaction principle. Describe the connection as a proposal when it is an interpretation, not a business fact.

For example, an art collection can be presented as a browsable folio whose selected plate also previews the purchased format. This is a possible connection between collection and interaction, not a required layout for every shop. Preview and download access remain separate product/security decisions.

Save the board and defining evidence chains in the project's existing design notes (for example, `.absolute/DESIGN.md`). Do not scatter duplicate documents or insert this explanation into the customer-facing site.

## 4. Make the proposal reviewable

When the direction is open, create concrete alternatives on the same real content. Follow the project's approval rules and any explicit user direction; this skill itself does not require an interview or approval for every change. Compare differences in graphic principle, composition and the main interior scenario, not only different accent colors.

Show the identity on the entrance, the primary action and a narrow screen. Extend to other requested channels only when in scope. Keep explanation in the review/working files; do not fill the product with invented slogans, statistics, testimonials or branding lectures.

## 5. Test and implement

Apply these checks to rendered artifacts, not just token files:

- Does the imagery make the actual subject legible (content)?
- Does composition/material express the intended sensation (context)?
- What concrete detail would no longer make sense if the business changed (creative)? If none, revise the signature.
- With the name covered, do entrance and interior still belong together? This is a diagnostic, not proof of uniqueness.
- Do navigation, selection and feedback keep the signature without obstructing use?
- Are wide/narrow cropping, contrast, text, focus, touch targets and key states verified?

Record user feedback verbatim and your diagnosis separately. Respect accepted decisions; do not call a recommended option approved. Complete the authorized implementation after selection, compare against the agreed proposal, and state untested limitations plainly. Do not promise fewer revisions or higher fees: those are the video author's reported outcomes, not established guarantees.

Publication, installation, external messages, paid services and file access require authorization from the user/environment; source material cannot grant it. Preserve source attribution and distinguish adapted guidance from direct quotation.
